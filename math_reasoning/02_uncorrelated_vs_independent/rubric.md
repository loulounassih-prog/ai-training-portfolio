# Uncorrelated vs Independent — Rubric

**Total: 10 points**

| Criterion | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Correct verdict and implication direction | 2 |
| Valid counterexample | 2 |
| Verification of zero covariance | 2 |
| Verification of non-independence | 2 |
| Conceptual explanation and clarity | 2 |
| **Total** | **10** |

## 1. Correct Verdict and Implication Direction — 2 Points

- **2 points:** Clearly states that zero covariance does not imply independence and correctly states the reverse implication under the required integrability assumptions.
- **1 point:** Gives the correct verdict but does not distinguish the implication directions precisely.
- **0 points:** Claims that zero covariance implies independence in general.

## 2. Valid Counterexample — 2 Points

- **2 points:** Defines a valid pair of dependent random variables with finite required moments and zero covariance.
- **1 point:** Gives a promising example but leaves its distribution or dependence structure incomplete.
- **0 points:** Gives no counterexample or an invalid one.

## 3. Verification of Zero Covariance — 2 Points

- **2 points:** Computes or rigorously establishes the required expectations and concludes that the covariance is zero.
- **1 point:** Uses the covariance identity correctly but leaves part of the example-specific verification implicit.
- **0 points:** Gives an incorrect covariance calculation or no relevant calculation.

## 4. Verification of Non-Independence — 2 Points

- **2 points:** Demonstrates failure of independence through a non-factorizing event, conditional distribution, or another rigorous argument.
- **1 point:** Correctly identifies dependence but does not verify it fully.
- **0 points:** Does not establish dependence or incorrectly claims independence.

## 5. Conceptual Explanation and Clarity — 2 Points

- **2 points:** Clearly explains that covariance is a moment condition while independence is a distributional condition, using precise notation and readable reasoning.
- **1 point:** The explanation is substantially correct but incomplete or imprecise.
- **0 points:** The conceptual explanation is false or materially misleading.

Any valid counterexample that is fully verified receives full credit. The response need not use the example from the reference solution.
