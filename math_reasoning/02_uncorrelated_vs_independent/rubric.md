# Rubric

Total: 10 points

## 1. Correct verdict: zero covariance does not imply independence — 2 points

- 2 pts: Clearly states that $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=0$ does not imply independence.
- 1 pt: Suggests the statement is not always true but is vague.
- 0 pts: Claims that zero covariance implies independence.

## 2. Provides a valid counterexample — 2 points

- 2 pts: Uses a correct counterexample such as $X$ uniform on $\{-1,0,1\}$ and $Y=X^2$.
- 1 pt: Gives an example that suggests the right idea but is incomplete or not fully specified.
- 0 pts: Gives no counterexample or gives an invalid one.

## 3. Correctly shows non-independence — 2 points

- 2 pts: Rigorously verifies non-independence using probabilities, for example by showing

$$
\mathbb{P}(X=0,Y=0)\neq \mathbb{P}(X=0)\mathbb{P}(Y=0).
$$

- 1 pt: States that $Y$ depends on $X$ but does not verify it rigorously.
- 0 pts: Does not show non-independence or incorrectly claims independence.

## 4. Correctly computes the covariance — 2 points

- 2 pts: Correctly computes $\mathbb{E}[X]$, $\mathbb{E}[Y]$, $\mathbb{E}[XY]$, and concludes $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=0$.
- 1 pt: Gives the right covariance conclusion but with incomplete computation.
- 0 pts: Computes the covariance incorrectly.

## 5. Explains the conceptual distinction — 1 point

- 1 pt: Clearly explains that zero covariance is a linear moment condition, while independence is a stronger distributional condition.
- 0 pts: Does not explain the distinction.

## 6. Clarity and mathematical presentation — 1 point

- 1 pt: The solution is clear, well-structured, and uses correct notation.
- 0 pts: The solution is hard to follow, poorly formatted, or mathematically ambiguous.
